You heard the historian: *going to a certain website is the easiest way to see what someone else said without being assaulted with ads.*

That sentence should make you question citations entirely — because it reveals that the footnote and the reading experience have drifted apart.

## From shelf to string

`025.431` worked because a library was a **single implicit universe**. The building answered *"in what universe?"* for free, so the string only had to encode *which shelf*.

On the open web, the string became ritual punctuation — and the most important fact (the spoken grant at `3:02`) has no slot in any of it.

## Your door

The ad-free projection is the door. [`/bare/`](/bare) is zero CSS, zero JS, zero ads — pure event. Record your consumption surface honestly, and project to Chicago 18 Notes & Bibliography without losing provenance:

| What you cite | What you record |
| --- | --- |
| the event | the utterance on its own clock |
| the projection | `youtube` / `youtube_music` / `/bare/` |

The citation is the event; the medium of consumption is the projection. You can change your viewing angle whenever you want — the event does not move, and neither does the archive's duty to say which glass you looked through.
