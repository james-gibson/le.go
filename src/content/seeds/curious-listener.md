You don't need to know what a citation is to start here. You just need to have felt the small absurdity of it.

## Start with bricks

A classic citation is a **glued model**. Someone took five real facts — who made it, what it's called, when, where, and the URL — squirted punctuation glue over them, and handed you a rigid plastic lump:

```text
SikMadMan. (2026). Open Arms [Video]. YouTube.
```

Try snapping a timestamped remix licence onto that. You can't. The glue has set.

In `le.go`, every fact is a **studded brick** with standard clutch power. Snap them together into a complex-space universe, then unsnap and re-project them into Chicago, MLA, or APA at will — without stepping on a bare magic string in the dark.

## Why this song?

*Open Arms* is the rare work whose **reuse terms are spoken on the recording**. At `3:02`, the artist says:

> "You can use the music how you want… Sample this and remix that. Cut it up and bring it back. Credit would be nice if you do."

That sentence is the whole point: the grant is *inside* the work, and no citation style has a slot for it.

## The four snaps

1. **`null`** — a missing brick is not a brick.
2. **`not null`** — the empty spot is information.
3. **`0 > 1`** — a brick with no studs joins nothing, but looks like it should.
4. **`42 = 0`** — every build starts from the same corner.
